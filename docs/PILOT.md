# Four-cycle shadow pilot (proposed, not performed)

Five discovery questions:

1. Who owns the Monday payroll estimate and Friday reconciliation?
2. Which accounting report and timestamp establish available cash, and what outgoing obligations does it already include?
3. How are completed work, approved change orders, retainage, disputes and credits recorded today?
4. Which invoice identifiers survive exports, project renames and partial payments?
5. Which warning would change an office manager's next action, and who approves that action?

Before trial, time one full current weekly routine: collect balances, estimate payroll, reconcile project actuals, identify billing/collection delays, and produce the owner's brief. Record minutes per step, record counts, missing inputs and unresolved differences. Do not invent a time-saving baseline.

For four Friday payroll cycles, use synthetic data first, then only separately authorized customer records in a secured environment. Keep the existing accounting/payroll process authoritative. Each Monday enter estimates; each Friday reconcile actuals and snapshot evidence. Compare exported figures line-by-line to accounting. Log entry minutes, discrepancies in cents, stale/missing inputs, warnings judged actionable and follow-up outcomes. Do not send collection messages automatically.

Proposed success criteria: all four briefs reconcile to source with zero unexplained cents; duplicate imports leave totals unchanged; every displayed amount has traceable inputs; missing payroll never becomes zero; no recorded-cash changes from receivables; the office manager can explain the bridge without assistance. Treat an entry-time reduction of at least 20% and at least one actionable warning per cycle as hypotheses to evaluate, not proven benefits.

Stop on unexplained cash/payroll discrepancies, lost amendments, duplicate counting, cross-organization exposure, or mistaken reliance on expected receipts as available cash. Revert to the authoritative accounting routine and repair before resuming. Repeated low-action warnings or longer entry time trigger workflow redesign.

Next-scope decision after four cycles: continue only if reconciliation and usefulness pass. Choose one validated friction point (e.g. a specific accounting CSV adapter or dated credit-note support). Buyer, price, demand, willingness to switch and time savings remain untested hypotheses. Bank connections and payroll execution remain out of scope.
